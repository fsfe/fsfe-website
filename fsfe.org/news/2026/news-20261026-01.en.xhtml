European teenagers build their own tech: 6 winners of programming competition awarded

More than 85 teenagers from 21 countries took part in Youth Hacking 4 Freedom 2026, with six winners who created solutions ranging from a scientific graphing calculator to tools tackling food waste and decentralised search. Their creations show how young Europeans can build technology everyone can control.

Organised annually by the Free Software Foundation Europe (FSFE), Youth Hacking 4 Freedom (YH4F) allows European teenagers, aged 14 to 18, to bring their own ideas to life, with only one rule: the software must allow anyone to use it for any purpose, to study how it works, to share it with others, and to improve it. In a world where controlling our own technology is crucial, those skills are essential for society. Beside that, YH4F gives young people the chance to tinker, have fun with programming, and connect with like-minded peers.

More than 85 teenagers from 21 countries took part, with projects that stood out for their creativity, technical skill, and variety of ideas. The 2026 winning projects are a graphic calculator, a decentralised search engine, a solution for reducing food waste, a project supporting local communities, a tool that lets users build and own their trackers using existing networks, and a music-learning tool – all of them respecting users' rights to control technology.

We tend to look to Silicon Valley when we talk about excellency and technological innovation, but YH4F shows us that here in Europe, there are young people capable of creating extraordinary things. You can see what they accomplished at this young age and with little resources, so I am confident there are many more young people here in Europe who could be able to build technology that we, as society, can fully control”, says Matthias Kirschner, President of the FSFE.

2026 Winners

The six winning projects are:

  • Ultimate Hacker Award (awarded with €4,096 cash prize)
    Juan Ramón (Spain)
    Juan Ramón won the Ultimate Hacker Award with NumOS, a Free Software firmware and operating system for a Free Software scientific graphing calculator.

  • Elite Hacker Award (awarded with €2,048 cash prize)
    Simon (Germany)
    Simon received the Elite Hacker Award for FindMyWeb, a project that enables users to build and own their own trackers using existing networks, without relying on proprietary, vendor-specific devices.

  • Awesome Hacker Award (awarded with €1,024 cash prize)
    Konstantinos (Greece)
    Konstantinos won the Awesome Hacker Award with SmartFoodSave, an LLM-assisted platform designed to reduce food waste in school meal operations. The project combines data collection, machine-learning predictions, semantic meal normalisation, nutrition-aware menu planning, and donation-point discovery in a single full-stack system.

  • Best Arts Project (awarded with €1,024 cash prize)
    Marius (Germany)
    Marius won the Best Arts Project award with Pianove , a Free Software piano-learning application that works entirely offline, without an account or subscription. Users can practice pieces from MusicXML files using a MIDI keyboard. The application is built in Python using Pygame.

  • Best Community Support Project (awarded with €1,024 cash prize)
    Tommaso (Italy)
    Tommaso received the Best Community Support Project award for Needy, a secure, geolocated, self-hostable platform that connects people who need help with volunteers willing to offer their time. Users can request support with tasks such as grocery shopping, companionship, and small repairs, while volunteers can earn "Reputation Hours" in return.

  • Best Teamwork Project (awarded with €1,024 cash prize)
    Stella, David, Florian, and Mattis (Germany)
    The Best Teamwork Project award went to RastiSearch, a federated search engine designed to operate independently of major technology companies' indexes. The project emphasises decentralisation, integration with Tor, accessible self-hosting, and tools that make it easier for users to build their own web crawlers.

"These projects all began with a participant who saw something they wanted, or found frustrating, and decided to build it themselves. Along the way, the participants learned new skills and had fun programming. The result is Free Software that solves a real problem and that others can use, study, share, and modify. That is what Youth Hacking 4 Freedom is about: young people learning by building the tools they need and sharing them with a community that helps them grow. We are deeply grateful to our sponsors, whose support makes this competition possible. Without them, we could not spark curiosity about Free Software in the next generation”, explains Bonnie Mehring, FSFE and YH4F Project Manager.

Empowering Europe’s next generation of developers

The Youth Hacking 4 Freedom competition has just concluded its fifth edition attracting young people passionate about programming. United by an interest in tinkering with technology, these participants represent a generation that understands the importance of creating, modifying, using, and sharing the software they depend on. By empowering young developers to contribute to software freedom, YH4F fosters a culture of openness, transparency, and digital independence.

This initiative is made possible through the generous financial support of our donor, Reinhard Wiesemann, and the award sponsorship of our platinum sponsor Proxmox; our silver sponsors Collabora, Nextcloud, and SUSE Open Source Network; and our bronze sponsor KIX.

The registration for the YH4F 2027 edition is already open to any young European between 14 and 18 years old. The programming period is from 01.01.2027 to 30.06.2027. Details for the next edition can be found at yh4f.org.
