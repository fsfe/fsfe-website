2026 Winners

The six winning projects, created by participants aged 14 to 18 and presented at the 2026 awards ceremony in Brussels, are:

  • Ultimate Hacker Award: Juan Ramón with NumOS, a Free Software firmware and operating system for a Free Software scientific graphing calculator.
  • Elite Hacker Award: Simon with FindMyWeb, a project that enables users to build and own their own trackers using existing networks, without relying on proprietary, vendor-specific devices.
  • Awesome Hacker Award: Konstantinos with SmartFoodSave, a project that enables users to build and own their own trackers using existing networks, without relying on proprietary, vendor-specific devices.
  • Best Arts Project: Marius with Pianove, a Free Software piano-learning application that works entirely offline, without an account or subscription.
  • Best Community Support Project: Tommaso with Needy a secure, geolocated, self-hostable platform that connects people who need help with volunteers willing to offer their time.
  • Best Teamwork Project: Stella, David, Florian, and Mattis with RastiSearch, a federated search engine designed to operate independently of major technology companies' indexes.

You can find out more about the six inspiring projects here.

2025 Winners

The six winning projects, created by participants aged 14 to 18 and presented at the 2025 awards ceremony in Brussels, are:

  • Ultimate Hacker Award: Marius & Jonas with OpenLingu, a flexible, Free Software language learning platform that allows users to learn languages through interactive lessons while providing tools for content creators to build and share educational content.
  • Elite Hacker Award: Pingu & Tuxilio with custom rom list, a project that automatically fetches Custom ROM devices and versions and displays it on a page. If you're a developer, a file devices.json is generated.
  • Awesome Hacker Award: Mario with Scroll Wheel, a big high resolution scroll wheel inspired by Engineer Bo.
  • Green Robot Award: Vinícius with Smart Plant Watering Robot, a robot that can water your plants at home.
  • Beautiful Math Award: Corentin with Hyper – A Hyperbolic Rendering Engine for visualizing non-Euclidean geometry. It renders an infinite world, offering an Educational, interactive view of hyperbolic tilings and maze algorithms.
  • Thinking Out of the Box Award: Augustin with dcg which aims at being a fast distributed revision control system analysing files syntactically rather than linearly.

You can find out more about the six inspiring projects here.

2024 Winners

The six winning projects, developed by young people aged between 14 and 18 and presented at the 2024 award ceremony are:

  • Ultimate Hacker Award: Gaspard with Versia, a federated social network based on the Versia protocol.
  • Elite Hacker Award: Sofía with Identity, an app that helps people in palliative care or suffering from dementia to save their most meaningful memories.
  • Awesome Award: Eventfully, a team project by Konrad, Luis, Benedikt, Dorothea, Leonie and Jona: a software to find and explore events in your area, connect with others, and learn more about culture, technology and other topics from one website.
  • Creative Hacker Award: Manuel with YLLM4F (Youth Large Language Models 4 Freedom), a platform, that combining three advanced language tools, ensures that chatbots interact in a youth-friendly way.
  • Epic Game Award: Corentin, Edmond and Tipragot with Beats into shapes, a rhythm game where you forge items with the sound of music.
  • Community Support Award: Tobias with PraiseLink, a WebApp for sharing sheets and lyrics of music. It can be used for singing choirs or live performance groups.

You can find out more about the six inspiring projects here.

2023 Winners

The six projects that won the 2023 edition of Youth Hacking 4 Freedom are:

  • Ultimate Hacker Award: Davide with ArduPlot, a real-time plotter for microcontrollers that uses a custom protocol to send data to be displayed in different graphs.
  • Elite Hacker Award: Marlon with ClassQuiz, an interactive quiz platform for educators and presenters to create and host quizzes.
  • Awesome Award: Simon with OpenRadio, a Desktop app combining Digital Audio Broadcasting (DAB), internet radio and alarm into one single app.
  • Best Maker Award: Oriol with DogBag4City, an intelligent device that provides dog owners with small bags to collect their dogs' excrement.
  • Best Freedom Project Award: Marius with Artix Engine, a user-friendly 2D game engine written in Python, designed to empower non-programmers in bringing their game ideas to life.
  • Best Power User Tool Award: Matthias with hashfindutils, a program to efficiently search on your computer for a file whose hash you somehow already know.

You can find out more about the six inspiring projects here.

2022 Winners

In 2022, it was the first time YH4F took place. We had the pleasure of receiving many interesting and inspired projects from all around Europe. The six projects that won the 2022 edition of Youth Hacking 4 Freedom are:

  • Ultimate Hacker Award: Stavros with SignTrack, a project that aims to translate sign language to writing. It is a valuable technology for people who use sign language to communicate fast with an audience who do not know sign language.
  • Elite Hacker Award: Miquel with Smart Table Assistant, a robot-model intending to help people with disabilities in the lower extremities.
  • Awesome Hacker Award: Artur with Aspinwall, an easy way to turn old touchscreen devices into smart displays.
  • Ultimate Girl Hacker Award: Ekaterina with Music Companion, a program helping the user learn to play music.
  • Special Hacker Award: Mark with Sharik, a simple, cross-platform solution for sharing files within a local network.
  • Youngest Hacker Award: Héctor with LibreHomework, a tool to schedule tasks and get notified, write down exams, organize your documents and lock your screen to help you focus on your tasks.

You can find out more about the six inspiring projects here.
